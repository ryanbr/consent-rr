! Title: consent-rr - ConnectHolland CookieConsentBundle
! Description: Replaces ConnectHolland's CookieConsentBundle script for
!              Symfony with a stub that writes their own refusal in their own
!              cookies and takes their banner out.
! Expires: 7 days
! Homepage: https://github.com/ryanbr/consent-rr
!
! These filters do nothing on their own: the resources they name have to be
! installed through uBlock Origin's userResourcesLocation setting first. See the
! README.

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! ConnectHolland's CookieConsentBundle, a Symfony bundle - composer package
! connectholland/cookie-consent-bundle. Every Symfony app publishes a bundle's
! assets under the same path, so one rule by path covers all of them:
!
!   <any site>/bundles/chcookieconsent/js/cookie_consent.js
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
/bundles/chcookieconsent/js/cookie_consent.js$script,redirect=chcookieconsent-reject.js

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! THEIR SERVER WRITES THE CONSENT, which makes this family a different shape
! from the rest of this repo. Their script stores nothing: it posts the
! banner's form over XHR, and the Set-Cookie headers on that response are the
! record. Their Twig helper then decides server-side, at render time, whether
! a tracker goes into the page at all:
!
!   {% if cookie_consent_is_category_allowed('analytics') %}
!
! so there is nothing parked in the page for an un-block to free, and the
! refusal has to be in their cookies before the next render. Their own
! Cookie/CookieHandler.php and Enum/CookieNameEnum.php:
!
!   Cookie_Consent              date('r')
!   Cookie_Consent_Key          the visitor's existing key, else uniqid()
!   Cookie_Category_<category>  the string true or false
!
! a year out, path /, host-only, SameSite=Lax - and NO Secure flag, even on an
! https page, which is measured rather than read: their Symfony Cookie takes a
! null secure flag, which resolves to the request as the app sees it, and
! theirs is behind a proxy. What their server sets on the live page:
!
!   Cookie_Category_analytics  false                            HttpOnly  Lax
!   Cookie_Consent             Tue, 06 Oct 2026 08:00:20 +0000  HttpOnly  Lax
!   Cookie_Consent_Key         6ac4aa94b9c7f                    HttpOnly  Lax
!
! so no Secure here either - a cookie without it is sent on both schemes, so
! their server reads the refusal either way - and the date goes in in UTC like
! theirs rather than in the visitor's zone. Their gate is an exact compare with
! the string true.
!
! THEIR YEAR IS NOT ALWAYS A YEAR. Their CookieHandler asks for P1Y in every
! released version and so does this, but a browser may cap what a page writes
! harder than what a server writes: Brave caps a cookie set from JavaScript at
! 7 days and one set by a header at 6 months, which is why their own Expires
! reads April rather than October. Nothing in a resource can change that, so
! where a browser caps, their banner comes back when the cap runs out and this
! writes the refusal again.
!
! The categories are a config list, so the page's own form is read for them:
!
!   <input type="radio" name="cookie_consent[analytics]" value="true">
!   <input type="radio" name="cookie_consent[analytics]" value="false">
!
! Their refusal button was clicked on the live markup with their own file
! loaded and XMLHttpRequest stubbed, and it posts
!
!   cookie_consent[analytics]=false&cookie_consent[tracking]=false
!   &cookie_consent[marketing]=false&cookie_consent[_token]=...
!   &cookie_consent[use_only_functional_cookies]=
!
! which is exactly what the cookies above are written from.
!
! To see it work:
!
!   [consent-rr] chcookieconsent-reject 1.0.0 cookie=Cookie_Consent written=5
!                blocked=0 refused=analytics,tracking,marketing removed=1
!                told=1 banner=none parked=none posted=none logged=none
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! IT CANNOT BE BLOCKED INSTEAD, and this one is worse than most. Their banner
! is server-rendered and their own template puts the script tag inside it:
!
!   {% block script %}
!       <script src="{{ asset('bundles/chcookieconsent/js/cookie_consent.js') }}"></script>
!   {% endblock %}
!   <div class="ch-cookie-consent ...">
!
! and their own form theme renders the buttons through Symfony's button_widget,
! which is type="button", not type="submit". Measured on the live page: both
! buttons are type="button". Their click handler is the only thing that submits
! anything, so with the file blocked the banner cannot be dismissed at all -
! and their stylesheet gives .ch-cookie-consent a z-index of 99999 with no
! position and no display:none, so it sits in the middle of the document.
!
! THEIR HTTP_ONLY DEFAULT is the one thing this cannot answer. The bundle's
! http_only option defaults to true, and no page is allowed to replace an
! HttpOnly cookie, so a visitor who accepted before installing this cannot be
! walked back from here - clearing the site's cookies is the only way. The
! console line says so rather than reporting a refusal it did not store:
!
!   written=0 blocked=5
!
! Nothing is posted, so their CookieLogger writes no cookie_consent_log row
! and stores no IP. Their stylesheet is left alone - it styles a banner that is
! no longer there - and so is the form's action, which on the site sampled is
! /cookies/save, a route of the site's own.
!
! ONE RESOURCE. An un-block has nothing to free, and an accept would be their
! Cookie_Category cookies set to true, which is simply consenting.
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
