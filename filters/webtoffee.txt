! Title: consent-rr - WebToffee GDPR Cookie Consent
! Description: Replaces the WebToffee GDPR Cookie Consent script - the version
!              that blocks scripts - with a stub that writes their own refusal
!              and takes their banner out.
! Expires: 7 days
! Homepage: https://github.com/ryanbr/consent-rr
!
! These filters do nothing on their own: the resources they name have to be
! installed through uBlock Origin's userResourcesLocation setting first. See the
! README.

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! WebToffee's GDPR Cookie Consent for WordPress, the version with categories,
! an api and real script blocking. The legacy single-file version of the same
! plugin has its own list here - cookielawinfo.txt - and does none of those
! things.
!
! One rule by path: the file name is the same under both plugin slugs, and a
! site can serve it from anywhere. All of these are live:
!
!   www.cbu.edu/wp-content/plugins/webtoffee-gdpr-cookie-consent/public/js/cookie-law-info-public.js?ver=2.5.3
!   mahidol.ac.th/lib/webtoffee-gdpr-cookie-consent/public/js/cookie-law-info-public.js
!   <any site>/wp-content/plugins/cookie-law-info/public/js/cookie-law-info-public.js
!
! The second is why the rule does not name wp-content: that site serves the
! plugin out of /lib/.
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
/public/js/cookie-law-info-public.js$script,redirect=webtoffee-reject.js

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! For a site that withholds something until a category is on.
!
! example.com##+js(webtoffee-reject-unblock)
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! THEIR STYLESHEETS ARE LEFT ALONE, and this is deliberate rather than an
! oversight - three of them ship with this plugin:
!
!   public/css/cookie-law-info-public.css   the bar, the buttons, the
!                                           show-again tab
!   public/css/cookie-law-info-gdpr.css     their settings popup and its
!                                           accordion
!   public/css/cookie-law-info-table.css    the cookie-audit TABLE
!
! The first two style markup this resource removes, so they style nothing -
! blocking them would save one cached request and risk a site that reuses their
! classes elsewhere.
!
! The third is not consent UI at all. Their [cookie_audit] shortcode renders a
! table of cookies into a page - a live privacy page sampled carries seven
! cookielawinfo-row elements and seventy-six cli-tab classes - and that table is
! page CONTENT that stays after this resource has run. Blocking its stylesheet
! would leave a visibly broken table on the privacy page of every site using
! the shortcode.
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! THEIR REFUSAL, which is what this writes, verbatim from their reject button:
!
!   reject_close: function() {
!       this.hidePopupOverlay();
!       for ( var k in Cli_Data.nn_cookie_ids ) {
!           CLI_Cookie.erase(Cli_Data.nn_cookie_ids[k]);
!       }
!       CLI_Cookie.set(CLI_ACCEPT_COOKIE_NAME,'no',CLI_ACCEPT_COOKIE_EXPIRE);
!       ...
!       this.generate_user_preference_cookie();
!       CLI.generateConsent();
!   }
!
! so a refusal ERASES each non-necessary category cookie rather than setting it
! to no, and writes three records:
!
!   viewed_cookie_policy      no
!   cli_user_preference       en-cli-no-checkbox-necessary-yes-checkbox-
!                             analytics-no-...
!   CookieLawInfoConsent      base64 of
!                             {"ver":"1","necessary":"true","analytics":"false"}
!
! and their own toggleUserPreferenceCheckBox leaves the necessary category's
! own cookie as yes, which is what their reject ends with. Run beside their own
! 2.5.3 file on the same page, every one of those matches byte for byte for a
! fresh visitor.
!
! ONE DELIBERATE DIFFERENCE. For a visitor who had already accepted, their own
! reject button reads the checkbox states to build those two records - and it
! does not untick the boxes first, so it records analytics as "true" for a
! category it has just erased the cookie of. This writes the refusal in all
! three records instead.
!
! WHICH CATEGORY IS THEIRS TO KEEP is Cli_Data.strictlyEnabled, not the
! checkbox's disabled attribute: their own disableAllCookies unticks every box
! whose slug is not in that list, and the live payload carries
! [ 'necessary', 'obligatoire' ] - a translated site names it in its own
! language, and reading the attribute would write yes for a category their own
! reject erases.
!
! To see it work - a fresh visitor, then one who had accepted before:
!
!   [consent-rr] webtoffee-reject 1.0.0 cookie=viewed_cookie_policy=no
!                written=4 erased=0 categories=necessary,analytics,advertisement
!                surface=necessary removed=4 told=1 banner=none logged=none
!
!   [consent-rr] webtoffee-reject 1.0.0 cookie=viewed_cookie_policy=no
!                written=3 erased=2 categories=necessary,analytics,advertisement
!                surface=necessary removed=4 told=1 banner=none logged=none
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
! THEIR BANNER IS SERVER-RENDERED. There is no cli_show_cookiebar in this file
! and no prepend anywhere in it: the markup is printed into the page by PHP and
! their script only wires it. So the bar, the show-again tab, their settings
! popup and its overlay are taken out - a script that would have hidden them is
! no longer there.
!
! THEIR EVENT GOES TO THE DOCUMENT. Their AfterConsent dispatches
! cli_consent_update at document, carrying {status, categories:{accepted,
! rejected}}, and that is the only thing of theirs a page can listen for. It is
! fired here with the refusal.
!
! NOTHING IS LOGGED. Their saveLog posts the whole cookie jar to
! Cli_Data.ajax_url where their logging is on. Nothing here posts anywhere, and
! their admin-ajax endpoint is deliberately not blocked - it is WordPress's own
! and carries everything else on the site.
! -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
